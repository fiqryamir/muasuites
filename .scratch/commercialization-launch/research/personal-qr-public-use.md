# Personal DuitNow QR for public Client payments

> Research for commercialization-launch decision 11. Captured 2026-09-07 from first-party Malaysian sources only: PayNet, banks/acquirers, SSM, BNM and FMOS. This is a launch decision note, not Malaysian legal, tax or provider advice. Product terms, fees, limits and regulatory guidance can change.

## Answer

Do not override the map's beta-only personal-QR rule. A personal DuitNow QR or a personal mobile/NRIC/passport DuitNow ID is technically able to receive money from a Client, but the reviewed first-party sources do not establish a general provider permission to use a personal rail for ongoing commercial payments advertised on a public MUASuites page. PayNet presents those personal rails as personal transfers and presents a business QR or BRN-linked identity as the business route. The safe paid-launch baseline remains an SSM-linked sole proprietorship, a dedicated business account, and a provider-issued business DuitNow QR or BRN-linked business ID.

There is no first-party source found here establishing a universal ringgit threshold that converts an allowed personal receipt into a prohibited one. The relevant distinction is intended use, account identity, provider terms and onboarding, not transaction size. A technically successful transfer is not evidence that the provider has approved the use case.

## What the rails are

### Personal QR and P2P identity

- PayNet's personal DuitNow QR page says an individual can pay, send or receive money by sharing a QR, and describes receiving money by having another person scan the recipient QR and enter an amount. It gives a personal limit of up to RM50,000 per transaction, shared with third-party transfers, and says personal QR payments are free. Source: [PayNet, DuitNow QR for You](https://www.paynet.my/personal-solutions/duitnow-qr.html).
- PayNet's personal DuitNow Transfer page describes sending money to friends, family or anyone using a mobile number or personal DuitNow ID. It identifies mobile number, MyKad/NRIC, passport, army or police number as personal IDs. It says transfers up to RM5,000 are free and that higher transfers may attract a 50 sen fee, while provider terms still apply. Source: [PayNet, DuitNow Transfer for You](https://www.paynet.my/personal-solutions/duitnow-transfer.html).
- CIMB's current personal DuitNow page likewise documents a customer receiving money by showing a QR and registering a mobile number, NRIC, passport or other personal ID, while separately describing scanning a merchant QR to pay a local merchant. Source: [CIMB Clicks, DuitNow](https://www.cimbclicks.com.my/content/duitnow.html).
- RHB says a personal customer can link a mobile number, NRIC, passport number or BRN to an RHB savings/current account, but its page distinguishes the BRN as the business identifier. It gives a combined daily limit of RM50,000 for several transfer methods, including DuitNow QR, and says there are no fees for DuitNow transfers on its service. Source: [RHB, DuitNow FAQ](https://www.rhbgroup.com/others/faq/payments-and-transfer/duitnow/index.html).

These pages establish technical capability and provider-specific retail limits. They do not say that a personal account may be advertised as an ongoing merchant payment destination.

### Business QR and business identity

- PayNet's business DuitNow QR page describes the product as accepting customer payments for a business, tracking sales and reconciling payments. It says the business should contact a preferred bank or acquirer, that fees vary by provider, and that settlement, notifications and timing are subject to the provider's terms. Source: [PayNet, DuitNow QR for Your Business](https://www.paynet.my/business-solutions/duitnow-qr.html).
- PayNet's business DuitNow Transfer page names the Business Registration Number (BRN) as the business DuitNow ID and says an SSM-registered business can register it. It also says each verified ID can be linked to only one bank account, although multiple verified IDs may be registered. Source: [PayNet, DuitNow Transfer for Your Business](https://www.paynet.my/business-solutions/duitnow-transfer.html).
- PayNet directs a business seeking a DuitNow QR to a preferred bank or acquirer and lists participating providers. Source: [PayNet, DuitNow QR Merchant Sign Up](https://www.paynet.my/business-solutions/duitnow-qr-merchant-sign-up.html).
- CIMB's business page says its Basic Package is for sole proprietors and partnerships with one or two outlets, requires a CIMB Business Current Account/-i, provides a static DuitNow QR, accepts CIMB and other bank/e-wallet payments, has an unlimited daily acceptance limit, and publishes provider-specific MDR and settlement terms. Source: [CIMB, Merchant Solutions](https://www.cimb.com.my/en/business/business-solutions/solutions/merchant-solutions.html).
- CIMB's business current-account page expressly includes sole proprietorships/enterprises and lists business-account application, owner identification and initial deposit requirements. Source: [CIMB, Online Business Current Account](https://www.cimb.com.my/en/business/business-day-to-day/deposit-investments/current-account/online-business-current-account.html).

The distinction is therefore not that personal QR cannot technically accept money. It is that PayNet's documented business path gives the recipient a business identity, provider onboarding and merchant-oriented records and support rather than a person's retail identity.

## Permission versus common practice

### What is technically possible

Personal QR and personal IDs can receive a transfer when the sender scans or enters them. A sole proprietor may also personally own the business, so money arriving in an account controlled by that person may be real business revenue. That explains why personal QR use can appear to work in small informal businesses.

### What is not established by that fact

- PayNet's personal product pages do not grant permission for ongoing public commercial use.
- PayNet's business pages do not say a business may opt out of provider business onboarding and use a personal QR instead.
- The reviewed bank pages and terms do not provide a general statement that a retail account's personal QR may be placed on a public business booking page for recurring service payments.
- No universal personal-versus-business transaction amount or number-of-payments exception was found.

The conclusion is intentionally narrower than saying every personal receipt is unlawful. Common practice, an accepted transfer, a low transaction amount or a customer who is known personally does not answer the account provider's intended-use and monitoring requirements. MUASuites should not turn informal practice into a public product default.

## Payer-visible identity

- PayNet says a payer should use a registered DuitNow ID and receive funds into the linked account. The business route can use a BRN, while the personal route uses a mobile number, NRIC, passport or similar personal ID.
- GXBank's retail terms say the bank may perform a name enquiry against the DuitNow ID National Addressing Database to verify or identify the name associated with the registered ID. Source: [GXBank, Retail Banking Terms, effective 30 January 2026](https://www.gxbank.my/terms-and-conditions).
- RHB tells a payer to check the DuitNow ID and verify the recipient's name before confirming. Source: [RHB, DuitNow FAQ](https://www.rhbgroup.com/others/faq/payments-and-transfer/duitnow/index.html).
- GXBank's business terms similarly provide for a name enquiry and define a business DuitNow ID in the context of business onboarding. Source: [GXBank, Business Banking Terms, effective 9 March 2026](https://www.gxbank.my/business-terms-and-conditions).

The exact name and formatting shown in a payer's bank or e-wallet app are provider-specific and must be confirmed before launch. A personal rail should be assumed to expose the individual's bank-verified identity, not the MUA's studio name. Publishing a personal NRIC/passport identity on a public page is not an acceptable workaround. A mobile number may also expose personal contact information. A business QR or BRN-linked ID is the correct way to make the payment destination's identity correspond to the registered business, subject to the provider's display rules.

## Records and reconciliation

Personal rails do produce bank-side transaction records, but they do not by themselves provide a merchant ledger tied to a booking, Client, deposit/balance, refund or service. This matters even where the MUA is a sole proprietor:

- GXBank's retail terms provide for periodic statements and transaction records, but also require the customer to check statements and notifications and allow correction, reversal or cancellation of entries in specified circumstances. Source: [GXBank, Retail Banking Terms](https://www.gxbank.my/terms-and-conditions).
- GXBank's business terms provide for business-account statements, transaction notifications and retained records for legal and operational purposes. They also say a business account is solely for conducting business activity and not for individual usage. Source: [GXBank, Business Banking Terms](https://www.gxbank.my/business-terms-and-conditions).
- PayNet describes consolidated statements and real-time sale tracking as business QR benefits, while warning that provider terms control the actual service. Source: [PayNet, DuitNow QR for Your Business](https://www.paynet.my/business-solutions/duitnow-qr.html).

For any beta exception explicitly approved by a provider, the MUA would still need a manual ledger linking at least booking/reference, Client, amount, date/time, payment type, bank transaction reference, proof, MUA confirmation, refund and correction history. MUASuites must not represent a screenshot or personal account notification as platform verification of settled funds. A business QR is not automatically a full accounting system either; the MUA must confirm the provider's statement/export and retention features.

## Limits, fees and settlement

Personal and business figures are not interchangeable:

- PayNet advertises personal QR receipt/payment as free and gives up to RM50,000 per personal QR transaction. Personal DuitNow Transfer pricing and limits depend on the provider; PayNet's personal page advertises free transfers up to RM5,000 and a possible 50 sen fee above that.
- RHB publishes a combined daily transfer limit of RM50,000 and no fee for its personal DuitNow service. This is an RHB setting, not a PayNet-wide promise.
- PayNet says business QR fees vary by bank, e-wallet or acquirer and payments are typically instant subject to provider terms.
- CIMB publishes zero MDR for CASA and e-wallet DuitNow QR under its Basic Package, a separate credit-card MDR, and settlement timing based on the transaction time. These are CIMB package terms, not a universal business-QR rule.

There is no basis for using a personal QR merely because a service's individual payment amounts fit under a retail limit. Before onboarding a business route, confirm with the selected provider the incoming limit, daily/monthly monitoring, fees/MDR, settlement timing, static or dynamic QR behavior, notifications, and whether a separate merchant agreement is required.

## Disputes, refunds and mistaken payments

Personal P2P transfer is not a card payment or a merchant acquiring arrangement:

- CIMB documents the personal QR flow as scanning a recipient QR, entering an amount and confirming the transfer. Source: [CIMB Clicks, DuitNow](https://www.cimbclicks.com.my/content/duitnow.html).
- RHB tells the sender to verify the recipient name before confirming and, if the recipient says funds were not received, to check transaction history and contact the bank. Source: [RHB, DuitNow FAQ](https://www.rhbgroup.com/others/faq/payments-and-transfer/duitnow/index.html).
- GXBank's retail terms state that instructions are irrevocable and binding and cannot be cancelled or reversed unless the bank agrees otherwise. The terms separately permit adjustments or reversals for errors, wrongfully credited funds or other stated grounds. Source: [GXBank, Retail Banking Terms](https://www.gxbank.my/terms-and-conditions).
- GXBank's retail and business terms route complaints first to the bank and then identify BNM and the financial ombudsman route. Malaysia's current financial dispute service, FMOS, lists banking, Islamic banking and payment systems within scope, subject to its rules and member status. Sources: [GXBank, Retail Banking Terms](https://www.gxbank.my/terms-and-conditions), [FMOS](https://www.fmos.org.my/en/).

Accordingly, the MUA remains responsible for the Client's service dispute, cancellation and refund. A refund is a new action by the MUA, not an automatic reversal of the original P2P payment. The MUA needs a written refund process, proof of the original receipt, a record of the return transaction and a way to handle overpayments and wrong-recipient payments. A business/acquirer product may offer clearer support, but the selected provider's merchant terms must be checked; no chargeback, refund SLA or dispute outcome should be promised from the QR label alone.

## Sole proprietor and merchant onboarding

- SSM publishes the Registration of Businesses Act 1956 and the official registration guidelines, including new-business registration guidance. Sources: [SSM, Registration of Businesses Act 1956](https://www.ssm.com.my/Pages/Legal_Framework/Registration-of-Business-Act.aspx), [SSM, Guidelines for Registration of a Business](https://www.ssm.com.my/Pages/Services/Registration-of-Business-%28ROB%29/Registration/Registration.aspx).
- MalaysiaBiz identifies business registration as the government route for registering a business and applying for business licences. Source: [MalaysiaBiz, Business Registration](https://malaysiabiz.gov.my/en/services/business-registration).
- PayNet says a BRN is for an SSM-registered business. PayNet's business QR route requires contacting a participating bank or acquirer; it does not say that a Sdn Bhd is required.
- CIMB expressly supports sole proprietorships/enterprises for its business current account and separately requires its business current account for the cited merchant Basic Package.
- GXBank's business account is expressly available to Malaysian SSM-registered sole proprietors and its terms require due diligence and use of the business account solely for business activity. Sources: [GXBank Biz Account](https://www.gxbank.my/bizaccount), [GXBank, Business Banking Terms](https://www.gxbank.my/business-terms-and-conditions).

The practical answer is that an SSM sole proprietorship can be a sufficient entity form for this launch, but the MUA should register the relevant business activity, obtain any activity/location licence that applies, and complete the selected provider's business KYC/merchant onboarding. A personal DuitNow identity does not replace those steps. This research did not find a rule saying that every sole proprietor is legally barred from every personal receipt; it did find no safe basis to advertise personal rails as the production commercial route.

## Required launch rule

Keep the map rule: personal QR is beta-only and never the public client-payment default. For the paid public launch, require:

1. The MUA has an SSM-linked sole proprietorship or other reviewed business form and the relevant service activity/licensing check.
2. The MUA uses a dedicated account whose provider has approved the business use case.
3. The MUA uses a provider-issued business DuitNow QR and/or a BRN-linked business DuitNow ID, not a personal NRIC/passport identity.
4. The provider confirms in its current terms or in writing the QR/ID type, payer-visible name, fees, limits, settlement timing, record/export capability, fraud controls, account monitoring and business activity permission.
5. The page identifies the MUA/business as the direct payee, makes clear that MUASuites does not collect or verify service funds, and does not expose an unnecessary personal ID.
6. The MUA maintains the ledger, keeps bank records and proofs, confirms receipt explicitly, and owns Client disputes, cancellations, overpayments and refunds.
7. Malaysian legal/accounting review signs off the registration, consumer wording, tax/record treatment and privacy implications before paid launch.

A personal QR could be considered only for a strictly private, time-boxed test after the named provider confirms that the exact account and use case are permitted. Even then, it should not be displayed on a public MUASuites page or used as a public commercial default. If a MUA cannot obtain a provider-approved business route, exclude that MUA from live Client payment collection rather than normalize personal QR.

## Unresolved confirmations

The following still require provider or Malaysian professional confirmation:

- Whether a specific selected bank permits recurring commercial service receipts into its personal account or personal QR. No general permission should be inferred from the retail product UI.
- The exact name shown to a payer for the provider's business QR, BRN-linked ID and any trading/studio name.
- Provider-specific incoming limits, velocity monitoring, fees, MDR, settlement, statement/export and retention behavior.
- Mistaken-payment recovery, fraud investigation, refund mechanics and whether the provider/acquirer offers any merchant dispute process for the selected package.
- SSM business activity wording and any local licence relevant to makeup services, plus tax and recordkeeping treatment of receipts and refunds.
- Whether any future MUASuites flow would cross the boundary into collecting, holding, splitting, settling or verifying Client funds. That would require a separate Malaysian payment-services analysis.
