# 01 - What Malaysian obligations apply to the commercialization launch?

Type: research
Status: resolved
Blocked by:

## Question

Using current Malaysian primary sources, establish the obligations that the commercialization plan must account for beyond the existing paid-plan entity and tax checklist. Cover the SaaS provider's entity and registration choices, tax and invoicing thresholds, privacy/data-protection duties for MUA and client data, website and SaaS terms, consumer or electronic-commerce obligations, and any payment-related boundary created by displaying MUA DuitNow QR codes and collecting receipt submissions without holding client funds.

Separate facts from professional judgment, cite the source for each material claim, and identify the questions that must be taken to a Malaysian lawyer or accountant before paid launch.

## Answer

Findings: [malaysian-launch-obligations.md](../research/malaysian-launch-obligations.md)

Resolution: Keep the SSM sole-proprietorship route as the working launch baseline, subject to Malaysian legal and accounting review. The commercialization plan must add PDPA privacy notices, documented controller/processor roles, vendor and cross-border review, retention and subject-request handling, breach readiness, and receipt-file security controls. It must also provide clear MUA/client terms and preserve the direct-payment boundary: the client pays the MUA, while MUASuites does not collect, hold, split, settle, or commission client service funds. SST/e-Invoice treatment, data-controller registration, DPO applicability, electronic-trade rules, and payment-provider perimeter remain professional-review questions rather than settled legal conclusions.
