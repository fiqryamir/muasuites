# Product

## Register

product

## Users

**Makeup Artists (MUAs)** — primary users. Freelance professionals in Malaysia who rarely open a dashboard. Their workflow lives in WhatsApp, Instagram, and Telegram. MUASuites is invisible software that integrates into their existing habits, not a separate tool they need to learn.

**Clients / Brides** — end users. Access booking funnels exclusively via mobile invite links sent through WhatsApp. Their entire interaction is a 5-minute checkout flow on their phone.

## Product Purpose

Invisible micro-SaaS that handles the tedious parts of MUA booking — date availability, travel surcharges, deposit collection, receipt verification, calendar sync — so MUAs can focus on their craft instead of admin. The tool should feel like a natural extension of WhatsApp/Instagram/Telegram, never like "software" the MUA needs to separately manage.

Success: the MUA forgets the tool exists because it just works.

## Brand Personality

**Invisible, warm, hasslefree.**

- **Invisible** — The UI steps aside. Clients flow through checkout; MUAs receive Telegram notifications without logging in. The dashboard exists but is rarely needed.
- **Warm** — Human, not corporate. The rose-toned palette carries warmth without cliché. Typography is approachable (Inter with generous leading). Copy is personal and direct, not robotic.
- **Hasslefree** — Every interaction removes friction. No accounts for clients. No passwords. No learning curve. The booking funnel is self-guided; the dashboard is sparse by design.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS dashboards** — high-density, chart-heavy, sidebar-overloaded templates. MUASuites should never feel like the user needs to "manage" anything.
- **Cheap booking widgets** — Calendly-lite, Google Forms aesthetic, low-effort booking embeds. Every surface must feel worth RM30/month.
- **Overly feminine bridal clichés** — no pink sparkles, script fonts, floral motifs. This is a professional tool for MUA businesses, not a wedding planning site.
- **Template feel** — anything that reads as a starter template or default shadcn. Every component should feel bespoke to the brand.
- **High density** — generous whitespace, low information density. Nothing should feel crowded or overwhelming.

## Design Principles

1. **Disappearing act.** If the MUA notices the UI, it's too loud. The best interaction is one they didn't have to think about. Surface patterns that reduce dashboard visits: push notifications over login, deep links over navigation, pre-filled data over forms.

2. **Worth the subscription (RM30/month).** Every pixel must justify the price. Not through bling or decorative effects, but through polish — smooth transitions, thoughtful microcopy, correct defaults, mobile-first layout. A client's checkout experience is the primary quality signal the MUA sees.

3. **Mobile-first, thumb-friendly.** The client experience IS mobile. Dashboard layout is secondary. Touch targets ≥ 44px. Single-column flows. No hover-dependent interactions. Forms designed for thumbs, not mice.

4. **Warm, not sterile.** The palette (rose primary on warm off-white) carries humanity. Avoid gray-on-gray corporate minimalism. Words like "your", "you're all set", "review" over formal labels like "submission" or "processing".

5. **Integrate, don't isolate.** The product bridges WhatsApp, Telegram, and the MUA's calendar. Every feature should reduce context-switching. If it forces the MUA into a dashboard to do something a Telegram message could do, the design is wrong.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Mobile-first. All critical flows work on small screens with touch input. Contrast standards follow WCAG AA defaults. No hover-gated interactions. Tap targets sized for thumbs. The client funnel has no authentication barrier — invite links are the entry point.
